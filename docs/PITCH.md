# Pitching QLine to a barber

Written for the first shop in Nangal, but it works for the next ten.

The barber does not want software. He wants fewer people walking out and
less arguing about whose turn it is. Sell that; the app is only how.

---

## The one sentence

> "Your customers wait on their phones instead of standing here, and they
> come back exactly when it's their turn. You press one button."

If he asks nothing else, that is the whole product.

---

## Before you walk in

Set his shop up completely, on your phone, the night before. Walking in with
his own name already on the screen is worth more than any explanation.

- Create the shop with **his real name**, not "Demo Salon".
- Add his real services and real timings (Haircut 20 min, Shave 10,
  Haircut + beard 30 — ask a regular customer if you don't know).
- Add his chairs/barbers by name.
- Set the app to **Punjabi**.
- Print the poster from Settings → Printable QR poster. Take it with you.
- Put three fake customers in the line so the screen isn't empty.
- Charge your phone, and carry a second phone (or borrow his) to play the
  customer.

Go at a quiet hour. Mid-morning or mid-afternoon, never Saturday evening.

---

## The three-minute demo, in this order

Order matters. Show the pain, then the fix, then how little work it is.

1. **Hand him the poster.** "This goes on your mirror." Let him hold it.
2. **Scan it yourself**, standing in front of him, on the second phone.
   Type a name, a number, join. Takes ten seconds. Say: *"No app. Nothing to
   download. It opens like a website."*
3. **Show the customer screen**: number 4, two people ahead, about 25 minutes.
   "He can go home, have tea, come back."
4. **Hand him your phone** — the barber screen. Make him press **Next**
   himself. Don't press it for him. The second phone buzzes with
   *"ਤੁਹਾਡੀ ਵਾਰੀ ਆ ਗਈ"*.
5. **Stop there.** That is the product. Don't show settings, don't show the
   dashboard counts, don't explain the database.

Then be quiet and let him talk. Whatever he asks first is what he actually
cares about — that is what you build next.

---

## The words

**Punjabi (what to actually say):**

> ਭਾ ਜੀ, ਇੱਕ ਚੀਜ਼ ਬਣਾਈ ਹੈ, ਦੋ ਮਿੰਟ ਲੱਗਣਗੇ।
>
> ਜਦੋਂ ਚਾਰ ਬੰਦੇ ਬੈਠੇ ਹੋਣ, ਪੰਜਵਾਂ ਮੁੜ ਜਾਂਦਾ ਹੈ — ਠੀਕ ਹੈ ਨਾ? ਇਸ ਨਾਲ ਉਹ ਮੁੜੇਗਾ
> ਨਹੀਂ। ਉਹ ਆਪਣਾ ਨੰਬਰ ਲੈ ਕੇ ਘਰ ਜਾ ਸਕਦਾ ਹੈ, ਤੇ ਜਦੋਂ ਵਾਰੀ ਆਈ, ਫ਼ੋਨ ਤੇ ਖ਼ਬਰ ਹੋ
> ਜਾਊਗੀ।
>
> ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਕੁਝ ਨਹੀਂ ਕਰਨਾ — ਬੱਸ ਇੱਕ ਬਟਨ, ਅਗਲਾ।
>
> ਪੈਸੇ ਕੋਈ ਨਹੀਂ। ਇੱਕ ਹਫ਼ਤਾ ਵਰਤ ਕੇ ਦੇਖੋ, ਵਧੀਆ ਨਾ ਲੱਗੇ ਤਾਂ ਹਟਾ ਦਿਆਂਗੇ।

**Hindi, same thing:**

> भाई साहब, दो मिनट दीजिए। जब चार लोग बैठे हों, पाँचवाँ वापस चला जाता है।
> इससे वो वापस नहीं जाएगा — अपना नंबर लेकर घर जा सकता है, और बारी आने पर
> फ़ोन पर पता चल जाएगा। आपको बस एक बटन दबाना है। पैसे कुछ नहीं, एक हफ़्ता
> चलाकर देखिए।

**The three things never to say:** "software", "app", "system". Say
*"ਲਾਈਨ"* — the line. It's a line, on a phone.

---

## What to show if he asks for more

Only if he asks. Each of these answers a real worry:

| He asks | Show him |
|---|---|
| "What about the old man with a button phone?" | The **Add walk-in** button. He types the name, they get a number like everyone else. |
| "What if I press the wrong thing?" | **Undo** — fifteen minutes to take back a Done or a No-show. |
| "Two of us cut hair here." | **Who is free?** — each barber calls their own customers, and a customer can ask for one by name. |
| "They'll say I skipped them." | The line is on the customer's own screen, with a number. Nobody argues with a number. |
| "I close at 9." | **Close shop** stops new people joining; whoever is already in line keeps their place. |
| "My customers don't read English." | Switch the app to Punjabi in front of him. The notification arrives in Punjabi too. |

---

## Money

For this shop: **free, and say so plainly.** You are not being generous, you
need a real shop using it daily — say that too. Barbers respect straight
talk.

> "ਮੈਨੂੰ ਪੈਸੇ ਨਹੀਂ ਚਾਹੀਦੇ। ਮੈਨੂੰ ਇਹ ਚਾਹੀਦਾ ਹੈ ਕਿ ਇੱਕ ਅਸਲੀ ਦੁਕਾਨ ਵਿੱਚ ਚੱਲ ਕੇ
> ਦੇਖੇ। ਜੇ ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਫ਼ਾਇਦਾ ਹੋਇਆ, ਦੋ ਹੋਰ ਦੁਕਾਨਾਂ ਨੂੰ ਦੱਸ ਦੇਣਾ।"

What you ask for instead of money:

1. He uses it **every day for one week**, not just when he remembers.
2. You can come in twice that week and watch for twenty minutes.
3. If it works, he introduces you to two other shops.

Don't mention a monthly price yet. You don't know what it's worth to him
until you've watched a week.

---

## Objections, answered honestly

**"Net slow hai / what if the internet goes?"**
Be straight: it needs internet, his and the customer's. On a weak signal it
catches up by itself within twenty seconds. If the internet is fully down it
won't work — keep the notebook in the drawer for that day. Saying this
up-front buys you more trust than it costs.

**"I don't have time to learn this."**
There is one button. Setup took you ten minutes last night and it's already
done. Offer to stand in the shop for the first busy hour.

**"My customers won't bother."**
They don't sign up, download, or type a password — they point a camera at a
poster. And he doesn't have to convert everyone: the ones who do use it are
the ones who would have walked out.

**"What happens to my customers' numbers?"**
Only he can see them, nobody else, and they're used for one thing: telling
that customer their turn is near. You're not selling them anything.

**"Someone else will copy it."**
Not an objection from him — if *you* start thinking it, ignore it. Nobody
copies an app a barber in Nangal is using until it's making money.

---

## Do not promise

Say "not yet" clearly rather than nodding. Every one of these is a real
feature request worth writing down — but promising it now is how pilots die.

- No SMS. Notifications need the customer to allow them, and on an iPhone
  they only work once the page is added to the home screen.
- No booking an appointment for 4 p.m. QLine is a line, not a diary.
- No payments, no billing, no khata.
- No reports yet — today's count only, nothing about last week.
- No second staff login yet; one account per shop.

---

## What to watch during the week

This is the part that tells you whether you have a business. Don't ask him
"did you like it?" — count:

- How many people joined by scanning, per day.
- How many no-shows, compared to what he remembers.
- Did he actually press Next, or go back to shouting names?
- Did anyone complain?
- Did he ask for anything twice? Build that.

If he stops using it by day three, the honest conclusion is that this shop
doesn't feel the problem hard enough — find a busier one. That is a result,
not a failure.

---

## The close

> "ਪੋਸਟਰ ਸ਼ੀਸ਼ੇ ਤੇ ਲਾ ਦਿਓ। ਮੈਂ ਕੱਲ੍ਹ ਸ਼ਾਮ ਨੂੰ ਆ ਕੇ ਦੇਖਦਾ ਹਾਂ ਕਿ ਕਿਵੇਂ ਚੱਲਿਆ।"

Put the poster up yourself, with his permission, before you leave. A pitch
that ends with something stuck to the mirror is a pitch that started.
